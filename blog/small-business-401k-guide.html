401(k) & ERISA

Offering a 401(k) at Your Small Business: A Straightforward Guide

By Benjamin R Gialloreto, CFA · June 15, 2026 · 8 min read

If you run a growing business, offering a retirement plan can help you attract and keep good people — and there are meaningful tax advantages for the business and its owners. The catch most owners worry about is complexity. Handled well, it doesn’t have to be.

Why offer a plan at all?

  • Talent: a 401(k) is table stakes for many candidates comparing offers.
  • Tax efficiency: employer contributions are generally deductible, and tax credits may offset start-up costs for new plans.
  • Owner savings: a plan lets owners contribute meaningfully toward their own retirement in a tax-advantaged way.

Your responsibilities as a plan sponsor (ERISA basics)

When you sponsor a plan, you take on a fiduciary duty under ERISA to run it in the best interest of participants. In practice that means prudent investment options, reasonable fees, and clear documentation. A plan adviser helps you meet those duties instead of guessing at them.

What we help with

  • Designing the plan (match structure, eligibility, vesting) around your goals.
  • Building and monitoring a prudent, low-cost investment lineup.
  • Documenting a fiduciary process so you’re covered if questions arise.
  • Educating your team so they actually use the benefit — which is the whole point.

403(b) plans for nonprofits

If you’re a nonprofit or educational employer, a 403(b) plays a similar role with its own rules. The same principles apply: prudent options, reasonable costs, and a documented process.
