Fees & Value

How Much Does a Financial Advisor Actually Cost? A Plain-English Breakdown

By Ben R. Grant · July 15, 2026 · 7 min read

“How much does this cost?” should be the easiest question an adviser answers — and it’s a fair one to ask on the first call. Here’s how the common models work so you can compare honestly.

The main fee models

1. Percentage of assets under management (AUM)

You pay an annual percentage of what the adviser manages for you — often around 1% a year, sometimes less as balances grow. It’s transparent and scales with your portfolio, which keeps incentives aligned: the adviser does better when you do.

2. Flat or retainer fees

A set annual or project fee regardless of portfolio size. This can make sense for specific, well-defined work.

3. Hourly

You pay for time, like an attorney. Good for one-off questions; less suited to ongoing management.

4. Commission-based

The “adviser” is paid by product providers when you buy certain products. This is where conflicts of interest can hide — which is exactly why the fiduciary standard matters.

Fee-based vs. commission — and why “fiduciary” is the word to listen for

A fiduciary is legally required to put your interests first. A Registered Investment Adviser like BRG Financial operates under that standard. Always ask directly: “Are you a fiduciary 100% of the time?”

Five questions that reveal the true cost

  • Are you a fiduciary at all times, and will you put that in writing?
  • How exactly are you paid — and are there any commissions or third-party payments?
  • What are the all-in costs, including underlying fund expenses?
  • What do I get for the fee — planning, tax-aware guidance, ongoing management?
  • Can you show me a sample of how you’d report performance to me?

Cost only means something next to value. The right question isn’t “what’s the cheapest?” — it’s “what am I getting, and is it worth it?”
