Getting Started

The Real Cost of Waiting: Why Investing in Your 20s Changes Everything

By Ben R. Grant · August 1, 2026 · 6 min read

If you take one idea from this article, make it this: time in the market is the single biggest advantage a young investor has, and it’s the one you can never get back. Not stock picking. Not timing. Time.

The 10-year head start

Imagine two people who each invest $400 a month and earn about 8% a year on average. The only difference is when they start.

  • Avery starts at 25 and invests for 40 years.
  • Jordan starts at 35 and invests for 30 years — same monthly amount.

Avery contributes only $48,000 more over their life, but ends up with roughly double the balance at 65. That gap isn’t extra saving — it’s compounding doing the heavy lifting on the first ten years of contributions.

The first decade you invest is worth more than the last two combined. That’s why “start now, adjust later” beats “wait until I have it figured out.”

You need less than you think to begin

A common myth is that investing is for people who are already wealthy. In reality, the most powerful move for someone in their 20s is small and boring: capture your full employer 401(k) match, automate a modest monthly contribution, and let it run.

A simple first-year checklist

  • Contribute at least enough to your 401(k) to get the full employer match — it’s an immediate return you won’t find anywhere else.
  • Build a small cash cushion (a few months of expenses) in a high-yield account before taking on more risk.
  • Automate contributions so the decision is made once, not every month.
  • Choose a diversified, low-cost core and avoid tinkering.

Where a fiduciary adviser fits

Early on, the plan is simple and you can do a lot yourself. As your income grows, decisions get more nuanced — Roth versus traditional, how aggressive to be, how to layer in other accounts. That’s where working with a fiduciary who is legally obligated to act in your interest starts to earn its keep.
